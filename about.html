About STAEX

Built on the belief that defense should be built at home.

STAEX is a defense business model inspired by SpaceGen Aviation — proof that a lean, vertically-integrated aerospace company can compete with legacy defense supply chains on speed, cost, and innovation.

Our Story

Why the model exists.

Modern defense procurement is slow, fragmented, and expensive — stretched across vendors, borders, and bureaucracy. STAEX was conceived as an answer: a single, disciplined pipeline that takes a UAV platform from concept to flight-tested hardware without the delays of a scattered supply chain.

Inspired directly by SpaceGen Aviation's approach to indigenous aerospace manufacturing, STAEX applies the same principle to defense — design, build, and test in-house, so capability isn't held hostage by import timelines or third-party dependencies.

Mission & Vision

Sovereign capability, by design.

Mission — Deliver mission-ready, indigenously engineered UAV platforms that give defense forces faster, more affordable access to modern unmanned capability.

Vision — To prove that a focused, vertically-integrated aerospace business — not a sprawling contractor network — is the fastest path to defense innovation at scale.

What Drives Us

The principles behind every airframe.

Indigenous First

Every core system is designed to be built domestically, reducing exposure to external supply shocks.

Precision Engineering

Flight-test-driven iteration ensures every platform performs exactly as required in the field, not just on paper.

Speed to Deployment

A modular, shared-platform architecture cuts the time between concept and mission-ready hardware.

Achievements & Milestones

The journey so far.

Phase 01 — Foundation

Model Conceived

The STAEX business model is defined — a vertically-integrated defense aerospace company built on the SpaceGen Aviation blueprint for indigenous manufacturing.

Phase 02 — Architecture

Platform Family Designed

A shared design architecture is developed across five UAV classes — from compact FPV interceptors to a long-endurance tailsitter VTOL — reducing engineering overhead and unifying the product line.

Phase 03 — Validation

Prototype & Flight Testing

Airframe and flight-control prototypes move through iterative testing, validating the multirotor and fixed-wing platforms against real mission profiles.

Phase 04 — Positioning

Indigenous Manufacturing Pathway

A domestic component and manufacturing strategy is established, positioning the fleet for production without reliance on imported critical parts.

Phase 05 — Next

Scaling for Deployment

The roadmap ahead: scaling production capacity and pursuing defense partnerships to bring the platform family into operational service.
